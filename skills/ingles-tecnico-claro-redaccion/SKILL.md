# Inglés técnico claro / Plain Technical English

Help the intended reader understand a technical explanation or carry out a task accurately. This editorial style draws directly on ASD-STE100, developed for maintenance documentation. The standard has writing rules and a controlled dictionary. Here, choose the wording and structure that best fit the teaching purpose and the discipline. A rewrite with this skill does not establish formal conformity. [ASD Simplified Technical English Maintenance Group](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html)

## Apply it to the requested text

“Rewrite this handout in plain technical English” selects the style. “Write this handout in English” selects the language. “Make the second paragraph clearer” calls for an ordinary edit. A mention of ASD-STE100 as a subject of study is also just a topic. A request applies to the specified text or task unless the teacher explicitly chooses the style for the course.

For a combined request, load the skill responsible for the deliverable as well. In “Prepara una actividad y redacta las instrucciones del alumnado en inglés técnico claro”, the [activity skill](../actividades-preparacion/SKILL.md) handles the assignment, rubric, files and audiences. Apply this style to the student instructions as you write them. Reply to the teacher in Spanish. For course notes or a wiki, use the corresponding skill in the [catalogue](../../docs/CATALOGO.md). In a bilingual document, apply each requested style to its own passages. Follow the [language preferences](../../docs/LANGUAGES.md) and the primary task’s source, access and validation rules.

A request about the worksheet's style applies to the author's prose. Add a language requirement for students or award points for their writing style only if the teacher asks to assess it. Preserve the rubric's learning objectives and weights.

## Make choices that preserve meaning

- In instructions, identify the action, its object and any prerequisite. Put a condition where the reader needs it. Split steps when that helps someone perform them independently. Keep a condition and its exception together when splitting them would change their scope.
- In explanations, keep the reasoning visible. Use a consistent name for each concept. Preserve technical distinctions and literal labels that the learner must recognise. Introduce an unfamiliar term with a short explanation when the audience needs one. Passive voice can be precise when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.
- Check the revision against the source. Preserve quantities, units, inequalities, sequence, uncertainty, attribution and the difference between a requirement and a recommendation. Keep quotations exact. If a missing detail would change the action or claim, identify the ambiguity for the teacher. Sentence length and vocabulary depend on the reader.

For example, “In the event of a deviation exceeding 2 mm, a second reading must be recorded before the mean is calculated” can become “If the deviation is greater than 2 mm, record a second reading before you calculate the mean.” Both versions preserve the threshold and the order of operations. The source leaves other deviations unspecified. An added example that forbids a second reading at 2 mm would turn *if* into *only if*. Check illustrations as well as the rewritten passage. In study notes, “The two patterns may have a common cause” still needs *may*, even if a shorter wording sounds more decisive.

When a learner must decide what happens outside a condition, identify the words in the source that govern that case. If the source gives no outcome there, say that the outcome is unspecified. If a phrase such as “provided that” could be read in two ways and the distinction matters, keep the original wording and ask the teacher which interpretation the example should teach.

For a supplied passage, return the requested revision in the conversation. For a larger deliverable, integrate it into the main task. If the teacher asks about the plugin or next steps, use the [course help skill](../proyecto-docente-ayuda/SKILL.md).
